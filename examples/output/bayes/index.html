The idea

Imagine you get a positive result from a medical test. How likely are you to actually have the disease? Bayes' Rule helps us update our belief about a hypothesis (having the disease) after observing new evidence (a positive test result). It combines the initial prevalence of the disease with the test's accuracy.

Why it matters

Bayes' Rule is fundamental in statistics, machine learning, and artificial intelligence. In medicine, it helps patients and doctors interpret diagnostic test results accurately, avoiding misjudgments based solely on test sensitivity. It's crucial for understanding the true meaning of "positive" in screening programs.

The key equation

P(D|+) = P(+|D)P(D)P(+|D)P(D) + P(+|not D)P(not D)
This equation calculates the probability of having the Disease (D) given a positive test result (+).

Symbols

P(D)Prior probability of Disease[0, 1]
P(+|D)Probability of positive test given Disease (Sensitivity)[0, 1]
P(+|not D)Probability of positive test given NO Disease (False Positive Rate)[0, 1]

    Grounding

    What comes from the paper, and what is ours

    Stated in the paper

    • The probability that two subsequent events will both happen is a ratio compounded of the probability of the 1st, and the probability of the 2nd on supposition the 1st happens. (P(A and B) = P(A) P(B|A))
    • If there be two subsequent events, the probability of the 2nd b/N and the probability of both together P/N, and it being first discovered that the 2nd event has happened, from hence I guess that the 1st event has also happened, the probability I am in the right is P/b. (P(A|B) = P(A and B) / P(B))
    • P(A|B) = P(B|A) P(A) / P(B), where P(B) = P(B|A) P(A) + P(B|not A) P(not A).

    Limitation / common misunderstanding

    The base-rate fallacy is the tendency to ignore the prior probability (base rate) of an event and focus only on specific information. In diagnostic testing, this means overestimating the probability of having a disease after a positive test, simply because the test is "highly accurate" (high sensitivity), without considering how rare the disease is. As shown in Exploration 1, a 99% accurate test for a 1% prevalent disease still leaves you with only a 50% chance of actually having the disease if you test positive.
